Your target splits

Your first 50 is always the fastest. You dive in and you are fresh, so it comes for free. After that, hold the same time on every 50. Then bring the last 50 home faster: about 0.6 seconds in a 400, and about a second in an 800 or 1500.

    Rounded to a tenth, so they may add up a few tenths either side of your goal. Swim the numbers, not the total.

    The drop-off, checked after the race

    Check it once, after the race. Inside the number, you paced it well even if you missed the target. Outside it, you went out too fast.

    Going out faster than your target 50 is the most common mistake in a race. It feels good for one length and costs you the back half. Practise the target pace until it feels slow.

    How to approach your goals

    1

    Are you versatile?

    Add any other best times you have below. If one event is already faster than its goal, that event is your real strength and the whole ladder should be rebuilt from it. Big gaps in one direction tell you what to train.

    2

    Pick your next-phase goals

    Chase what is close. Anything within about 2% of your current time is reachable in one training block. Pick one or two, not six.

    3

    Set one stretch goal

    Something within about 5% is a season target. Further out than that is a longer project, and it is fine to name it, but do not judge your season on it.
